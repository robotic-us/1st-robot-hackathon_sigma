SIGMA · measurement report · simulation only

One constant carries the planner

A re-measurement of the three motion-choosing algorithms in SIGMA's infant-responsive cradle, over 1,200 simulated nights. The headline replicates. Of the two settings the result was supposed to rest on, one turns out to be worth 30 points by itself — and the explanation our own documentation gives for why does not survive the control that tests it.

40 paired nights per arm × 3 independent seeds (3, 11, 23) · same infant, same hidden temperament, only the chooser differs · every arm through the real CradleMachine · no robot, no ROS, no phorce — 97 s of pure simulation.

What was re-measured, and why

SIGMA chooses which of 26 sustained motions to rock an infant with. Three algorithms can make that choice: the evidence report's fixed escalation ladder, a taught reflex heuristic, and the DREAM-Chunk planner, which dreams every candidate forward through a learned comfort model and takes the best. docs/DREAM-CHUNK.md records the planner at -26.4% upset time against the ladder, and attributes that to two settings: the matcher's exploration bound, and an infant model that settles cumulatively rather than memorylessly.

The decision code has been edited since. Read with comments and docstrings stripped, the changes are telemetry — a trend field for the dashboard, an extra key in the plan snapshot, an unused candidate-restriction helper, and two infant inputs that default to off. None of it should touch a decision. Rather than trust that reading, everything below was re-run from scratch, and every comparison was run three times on independent seeds, because a single 40-night run turned out not to be enough to tell two of these claims apart.

The headline replicates

The planner measures -26.8% upset time against the ladder on the primary seed (95% CI -32.7 to -20.9, t = -9.2, p < 0.001) and -54.8% crying time. Across all three seeds: -25.4% (range -26.8 to -23.9). The recorded figures are -26.4% and -57.1%. That reproduces — and the ordering, planner then heuristic then ladder, holds on every seed.

One number did move. The heuristic's own margin over the ladder measures -16.2% (range -17.7 to -14.8) against the -21.0% recorded, consistently smaller. The planner did not improve; the heuristic got worse, which matters for the comparison that follows.

Every night, not the average

A mean over 40 nights can hide an arm that wins hugely on a few infants and loses on the rest. It does not here: the planner beat the ladder on 36 of 40 nights.

Against the heuristic the margin is smaller but it is no longer marginal. On the primary seed it measures -14.1% (t = -4.3, p < 0.001), and across the three seeds -11.0% (range -14.1 to -9.3), significant on 3 of 3. The documentation records -6.8% at t = −1.8 and says to quote it as a direction rather than a result. On this evidence it is a result. The gap widened mostly because the heuristic slipped, not because the planner gained.

Setting 1 — the exploration bound, and it is the whole thing

The planner ranks candidates by a learned comfort model and adds a confidence bound, c·sqrt(ln N / n), wide while a motion's evidence is thin. It is one line and one constant (DreamConfig.explore_c = 0.06). Setting that constant to zero changes nothing else: the same world model, the same dreams, the same ranking.

Without it the planner measures +4.1% (range +2.8 to +6.5) against the ladder instead of -25.4% (range -26.8 to -23.9) — a swing of 30 points that puts it on the wrong side of the baseline it is supposed to beat, on every seed. A planner with an honest world model and no reason to leave it is worse than a fixed ladder that never learned anything.

The explanation for setting 1 does not survive its control

Our documentation explains the bound as forced rotation: an infant habituates, so a motion played until it stops working must be replaced whether or not it is still best-ranked, and it cites the heuristic — which rotates by explicit rule and gets its own large margin — as the parallel case. That is a testable claim, so this run tests it by removing the heuristic's rotation two ways.

Lifting the MAX_RUN cap does almost nothing (-17.6% (range -19.9 to -14.9) against -16.2% (range -17.7 to -14.8)), but that cap gates only the fussing branch, so it is the weaker of the two ablations. The stronger one pins the brain to its exploit path, removing the explore-while-fussing rule that does the real rotating. The heuristic then measures -16.8% (range -17.2 to -16.4) — indistinguishable from leaving it in.

So rotation is not a general explanation. Take rotation away from the planner and it collapses below the ladder; take it away from the heuristic and nothing happens. Whatever the bound is buying, it is something the heuristic's rotation rules do not supply, and the parallel drawn in the documentation should not be relied on. What the bound does for a planner specifically — one that would otherwise exploit a low-contrast learned ranking as though it were certain — remains the open question.

Setting 2 — the setting that is not in the algorithm

The second setting is a property of the infant, not of the planner. VirtualBaby(cumulative=True) integrates soothing into a settling score, so rocking that is working shows progressive calming and interrupting it loses that progress gradually. The older model stepped the infant down as a memoryless Poisson process; against that plant there is no trajectory to predict, and the documentation records the planner tying the heuristic exactly on it.

This is the claim that needed three seeds. On the memoryless infant the planner still beats the ladder comfortably — -18.3% (range -23.7 to -15.5) — which on one seed reads as the documented claim failing. Against the heuristic, though, the three runs measure -3.3% (range -10.0 to +7.4) and disagree in sign, significant on 2 of 3. On the infant with memory the same comparison is negative and significant on 3 of 3. Averaged over three runs the documented tie holds, and a single run of 40 nights was not enough to see it.

Did it replicate?

Each comparison, run three times on independent seeds, against the figure docs/DREAM-CHUNK.md records. Three dots is not a lot — but it is enough to separate a claim that lands in the same place every time from one that lands wherever the seed puts it, and two of these are the second kind.

What the numbers support, stated narrowly. The planner is better than the ladder, reliably and by about a quarter of the upset time. It is better than the heuristic on an infant that settles cumulatively, and that margin is now large enough to state. Almost all of it depends on one constant, and the mechanism our documentation credits for that constant is contradicted by its own control. The honest summary is that we know the bound matters far more than we know why.

What this does not show

  • Every number here is simulation. The infant is perception/baby.py, not an infant. Its habituation constants are the same ones the planner's world model assumes exist — though not the same values, which the policy never reads.
  • Three seeds is a weak replication. It is enough to catch a sign that flips; it is not enough to put a confidence interval on the spread between runs.
  • The exploit-only heuristic is a constructed control, not a flag that ships: it pins the rank the brain sees so the taught strategy always takes its exploit branch. The machine's own escalation and every safety decision are unchanged — but it is a larger intervention than setting one constant to zero, and the two ablations are not the same size.
  • The 2×2 varies one setting at a time against a fixed baseline. It is not a full factorial with an interaction term.
  • The safety layer is identical in every arm and is never ablated. The advisor only ever suggests a motion; CradleMachine validates it and keeps every safety decision.

Reproducing it

One command, no hardware:

python3 tools/ablation.py --nights 40 --seeds 3,11,23

Each arm runs through state_figure.run_night, so this page and data/states.html measure the same loop rather than two copies of it.
